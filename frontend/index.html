Find what moves

The computer tries hundreds of speeds and directions. For each one it lines the pictures up and looks for a dot that gets sharper. Speeds are in arcseconds (″) a year: Barnard's Star, the fastest star in the sky, moves 10″ a year.

    Each square is one motion. Bright means the pictures lined up into a sharper dot. Press a square to try that motion.

    More about this search

    Line up along a motion

    Type a motion. Every picture is slid back along it, then they are added up. Something that moved that way becomes one sharp dot; everything else smears out.

        How to read the globe

        You look at the sky from outside, like a desk globe (the real sky is the inside surface). Grid lines: every 2 hours of right ascension (RA) and every 15° of declination (Dec).

        SPHEREx Time Cube

        Stack time

        Real NASA telescope images of one patch of sky, stacked through time, so things that move stand out. Watch the star in the amber ring: Barnard's Star, six light-years away.

        The amber line is where star catalogues say it should be each year. The star stays on the line, which shows the pictures are lined up correctly.

        Why it moves

        A nearby object moves across the pictures for two reasons. It drifts in a straight line as it travels through space. And we watch from a moving Earth, so once a year it sways against the far-away stars. Hold up a finger and blink one eye, then the other: the finger jumps.

        The closer the object, the bigger the sway. Barnard's Star sways by a tenth of a pixel. A cold, dark planet far beyond Neptune would sway by dozens of pixels, and that is how it could give itself away.

        Your turn

        Open the same images in the full tool, as a 3D cube you can turn. Barnard's Star's motion is already known, so you can check the tool against it before you trust it on anything new.

        When the tool finds a drift that no catalogue explains, it calls it a candidate: not a discovery until other wavelengths and catalogues confirm it. These images are saved, so the cube opens without waiting.

        About SPHEREx Time Cube

        What this is

        Real NASA telescope pictures of one small patch of sky, stacked through time, so that anything that moves or changes stands out.

        Built for the 2026 NASA Space Apps Challenge, “Planet X and SPHEREx”.
